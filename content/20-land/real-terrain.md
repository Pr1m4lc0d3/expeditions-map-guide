Planned for this page:

- where public elevation data comes from, and what resolution is worth using
- **real terrain already contains its roads.** Bare-earth survey data captures a road as
  the physical thing it is, a cut-and-fill bench in the hillside. Gradient, switchback
  placement and cut depth are already solved for a real place
- getting orientation right, which is easy to mirror and hard to notice
- a practical check: overlay your imported roads against the imported terrain and
  confirm they agree. Checking each layer on its own cannot catch a disagreement
  *between* layers
- matching colour to a real place, and why a single online imagery source is not a
  reference
