Dressing the map

Music and sound

Background music is a table keyed by level name and can be added to a map that is already packed. Ambient sound cannot. How to tell which one you are holding, and the two pak members that decide whether anyone but you hears it.

Music and sound#

Music and ambient sound look like one feature and behave like two. Music is a lookup table the game reads at runtime. Ambient sound is part of the scene.

That difference decides how much work a change costs, so it is worth being able to tell them apart before you start.

The test#

pak.load_list inside a packed map is the engine’s index of that level’s resources. Count what appears in it. On a shipped map that plays four music tracks and has an ambient eagle call:

references to 'eagle'            28
references to 'magic_whispers'    0
references to 'music_presets'     0

The ambient sound is indexed. The music is not.

in the index in the scene needs the editor
music no no no
ambient or positional sound yes yes yes

So music can be added to a map that is already finished and packed. An ambient sound has a position, which means opening the map, placing it, saving, repacking, and getting it into the index.

The table#

Media/classes/sounds/music_presets.xml, one block per level:

<LevelMusic LevelName="level_my_map">
  <Sound Name="music/track_a" StartTime="24"   EndTime="6"    FadeInTime="30" FadeOutTime="10" />
  <Sound Name="music/track_b" StartTime="6"    EndTime="13"   FadeInTime="30" FadeOutTime="10" />
  <Sound Name="music/track_c" StartTime="13"   EndTime="20.5" FadeInTime="30" FadeOutTime="10" />
  <Sound Name="music/track_d" StartTime="20.5" EndTime="24"   FadeInTime="30" FadeOutTime="10" />
</LevelMusic>
  • LevelName is the level folder name, spelled exactly, including case.
  • Name is music/<slug> with no extension.
  • Each Sound owns a window of the in-game 24 hour clock. Midnight is written 24, as both a start and an end.

The windows have to tile the whole day. An hour no window covers is silence at that hour. If you only have one track, give it two windows, 24 to 12 and 12 to 24, rather than one window from 24 to 24, which is not documented and may mean the whole day or none of it.

A track shorter than a game day already loops inside its own window, so an extra restart at a boundary costs nothing you can hear.

Where the audio actually ships#

This is the part that catches people, because a map can sound finished in your own game and ship silent.

Media/sounds/music/<slug>.wav             your copy, for local play
<map>.pak        member  classes/sounds\music_presets.xml
<map>_pc.pak     member  sounds/music\<slug>.wav

Your Media folder is a development artifact. Somebody who installs your mod has the pak files and nothing else, so the audio has to be inside a pak or they hear nothing.

The main pak carries the table and no audio at all. The audio lives in the per-platform pak, in that platform’s codec: .wav for _pc, .mp3 for the PlayStation variants, .xma for the Xbox variants.

Format#

Every music track the game ships is 16-bit stereo PCM. Sample rate is not strict: shipped tracks include both 44.1 kHz and 48 kHz and both play. Length is not strict either, and the shipped ones run from 81 to 182 seconds.

Length costs download size rather than compatibility, because the pak member is uncompressed audio. At 48 kHz stereo 16-bit that is roughly 11 MB per minute before the archive compresses it.

Silencing music in one place#

<NoMusicSoundDomain> polygons in the level’s _sounds.xml mute music inside an area. That is the intended way to make one location quiet, rather than removing the level’s music and losing it everywhere.

After a repack#

A repack rebuilds the pak from scratch, so both members go away while the table still says the level has music. The symptom is silence with nothing else wrong, and nothing reports it.

If you script your packing, re-inject after every pack. The agent toolkit does this with install_music.py <level> --reapply.

Two things that will delist your map#

Both come from editing a pak directly, and both fail with no error at all.

Member names are not all spelled the same way, and the inconsistency is in the shipped files. Names under [...] use backslashes throughout, names under ui/ use forward slashes throughout, and everything else uses a forward slash first and backslashes after it. Copy the working spelling; do not tidy it.

Python’s zipfile rewrites backslashes to forward slashes when it reads an archive. On an untouched shipped map, 19 of 31 member names come back altered. Rebuild the pak from those names and the game can no longer find those members. The map then vanishes from the level list with no error, no log line and no crash dump.

Level paks are also padded to a multiple of 4096 bytes. One that ends flush is not listed.
