Placing the engine ghost

The see-through engine the game shows at start-up sits at the truck's engine sound, not its steam or its engine damage box. Why it floats out of the nose on a new body, and the tool that puts it in the bay.

This page is draft. It may be incomplete or unverified. Anything marked unverified has not been proven in a running game.

Placing the engine ghost#

Start a truck in Expeditions and a see-through engine flashes up in its engine bay. It shows again when the engine takes damage. On a truck you have built or brought in from somewhere else, that ghost often appears in the wrong place: out past the grille, floating above the hood, or 20 cm off to one side.

The fix is one line in the truck’s class file. The hard part is knowing which line, because the two obvious candidates do nothing.

What the game reads#

The ghost has three settings, and only two of them are named after it:

Setting Where What it does
EngineIconMesh <TruckData> which ghost model to draw
EngineIconScale <TruckData> its size
<Sounds Origin="(x; y; z)"> the truck’s <Sounds> element where it is drawn

The position comes from the engine sound. When the game loads a truck class it builds an object for the engine sound from <Sounds Origin> (and <Sounds ParentFrame>, if the truck gives one). The code that draws the ghost takes that object’s place in the world, pulls it a little toward the camera, and draws the ghost model there. Nothing else in the truck class sets it. The game’s own field list confirms this: the only fields with “EngineIcon” in their names are the mesh and the scale.

This was read out of Expeditions.exe with IDA after two in-game attempts had failed. The parser stores the ghost mesh and scale beside each other in the truck’s data, and the only code that reads them back is the drawer. The drawer positions the ghost from the engine-sound object, which the parser fills from <Sounds Origin>.

Two settings that look right and do nothing

<Steam Origin> places the radiator steam, not the ghost. Moving it left the ghost exactly where it was. Stock trucks put their steam all over the place: one stock truck’s sits 6 cm behind the truck’s own origin, two metres from its engine.

The engine <DamageArea> is the box that takes engine damage in a crash. On every stock truck it sits on the engine, which makes it look like the answer. It is not: moving it did not move the ghost either.

Coordinates are the truck’s own space, in metres: x forward, y up, z to the side. z is not always 0 on a ported truck: one ported Jeep carried a sound origin of (0.81; 0.779; -0.2), which is why its ghost sat 20 cm to one side.

Why a new body gets it wrong#

A truck built on another truck’s class keeps that truck’s <Sounds Origin>. If the old engine sat further forward or higher than the new body’s bay, the ghost follows the old engine. A truck brought in from SnowRunner can also carry a sound origin that was never meant for the ghost at all.

The ghost models#

EngineIconMesh names a mesh in the game’s shared.pak. Three are generic, modelled round their own origin so they can go in any truck:

Mesh Size at scale 1 (length x height x width) Reaches, from the origin
env/engine_small 0.66 x 0.64 x 0.56 m 0.28 back, 0.38 forward, 0.33 down, 0.31 up
env/engine_default 1.12 x 0.66 x 0.67 m 0.40 back, 0.72 forward, 0.43 down, 0.23 up
env/engine_big 1.47 x 0.66 x 0.95 m 0.48 back, 0.99 forward, 0.43 down, 0.23 up

The origin is not the middle of the model. The default engine reaches almost twice as far forward as back and sits mostly below its origin, so placing the origin “in the middle of the bay” puts the engine too far forward.

The other ghost meshes are named after stock trucks (env/engine_<truck>). Those are modelled in their own truck’s space, already in place, so on another body they land wherever that truck’s engine was. Use a generic one on anything you build.

A lift kit carries its own ghost

A suspension set can swap the whole truck for a truck replacement (classes/truck_replacements/<name>.xml). The replacement is a complete truck class with its own EngineIconMesh, EngineIconScale and <Sounds>. If your truck has one, place the ghost in both. That is also the only way a ghost can change with the build: the game reads the ghost from the truck (or its replacement), never from an engine add-on, so a V8 ghost can come with a lift kit but not with the V8 itself.

Putting it in the bay#

The engine-ghost tool in expeditions-tools does the measuring. It reads the truck’s own converted mesh out of the mod (no source files needed), finds the engine bay, and fits the ghost in it:

python engine_ghost.py --mod "<...>/Mods/.modio/mods/<id>" --truck my_truck --preview bay.png

It reports where the ghost is now and what is wrong with it (“pokes 0.04 m out of the nose”, “sits -0.13 m off the centre line”), suggests a <Sounds Origin>, and draws a side and plan picture with the bay, the hood line, the ghost now and the ghost suggested. --apply writes it into the mod’s pak (with the game closed), and --raise, --lower and --forward nudge it after you have looked.

How it finds the bay:

  • Nose: the front-most bodywork that reaches near hood height. Bumpers, winches and bull bars sit lower and further out, and a winch bumper once put one truck’s “front” 18 cm ahead of its grille.
  • Windshield base: the front edge of the glass near the centre line, from just under the hood up. Headlight lenses are glass too, but they sit lower and further out.
  • Bay: from 10 cm ahead of the windshield base to 15 cm behind the nose.

On the two trucks tested, the suggested front-to-back place landed within 2.5 cm of the place the owner settled on by eye. Height is a look, not a measurement. On those same two trucks the owner chose the ghost’s top about 25 cm under the hood line on one and 8 cm under on the other, so the tool’s height is only a starting point: check it in game and nudge.

Converted meshes do not agree on where a part’s vertices are

In some mod meshes a part’s vertices are stored relative to its frame’s whole parent chain (one truck’s body sat a metre too low read raw). In others they are already in truck space (another truck’s body landed 1.2 m outside its own mesh when the chain was applied). The tool takes, for each part, whichever reading keeps the most vertices inside the bounds in the mesh’s own header, which is the box the game itself uses.

What has been checked#

  • In game: <Sounds Origin> moves the ghost; <Steam Origin> and the engine <DamageArea> do not. Two trucks, one built from scratch and one ported, both placed and approved by eye.
  • In the exe: the drawer reads the ghost’s place from the engine-sound object, and no other field places it.
  • Not yet checked: whether <Sounds ParentFrame> makes the ghost ride a tilting cab. The stock trucks that set it use the cab bone, so it should, but nobody has watched it happen.
