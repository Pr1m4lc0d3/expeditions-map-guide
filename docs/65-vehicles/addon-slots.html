Add-on slots, dependencies and gear

How a new part reaches the garage. Slots on the truck, requirements on other parts, supply gear that adds fuel and spare parts, and the quiet failures between them, from a frame name ending in .001 to a socket offset the game ignores.

This page is draft. It may be incomplete or unverified. Anything marked unverified has not been proven in a running game.

Add-on slots, dependencies and gear#

A part you have built is not in the game until the truck has a slot for it and the part knows which slot it fits. Rock lights on any truck walks through the basic mechanism with a complete example. This page collects what goes wrong once a truck has many add-ons that depend on each other: a rack, gear on the rack, a tent instead of the gear, a lift kit that changes the truck.

Slots and requirements#

  • The slot is a <Socket Names="..."> in the truck’s <AddonSockets>. An add-on fits one with <InstallSocket Type="...">.
  • Sockets listed together in one <AddonSockets> group are one choice: fitting one add-on clears the others. Put a tent and the gear that would sit in its place in one group, and the player picks one.
  • An add-on that bolts onto another add-on needs <RequiredAddon Types="a, b" /> before its InstallSocket (any one of the listed add-ons will do). Without it, the garage offers fender flares on a truck with no fenders under them, and they float.
  • Ask “what does this bolt to?” for every new part, and write the requirement in the script that builds the part, not by hand in the pak. Hand edits are lost on the next build.

An add-on’s own Offset is ignored

InstallSocket takes an Offset, and it looks like the way to slide a stock part (a drone carrier, the metal detector) onto your rack. In game it did nothing: the detector sat on the ground in front of the truck. Put the offset on the truck’s socket instead (<Socket Names="..." Offset="(x; y; z)">). That worked, and the detector sat on the rack.

The frame name trap#

An add-on’s class names the mesh frame it draws (MeshFrame) and the frame it rides (ModelFrame). If Blender exports several add-ons in one session, it renames a reused helper object Bone_cdt.001, then .002. The converted mesh then carries BoneRackSlot1_cdt.001 while the class asks for BoneRackSlot1_cdt, and the part does not appear. The XML looks perfect.

When an add-on “does not work” and its XML matches a working one, list the frames in its converted mesh first. A script that exports many add-ons in one Blender session must delete its helper objects after each export, not just unlink them.

Mesh and class traps#

These each cost a round trip into the game, and none of them reports an error.

  • Never point a mod class at another truck’s mesh. A gear class that reused a stock truck’s tuning mesh (with its frames and a socket offset) froze the game at the loading screen: no log, no crash dump, nothing in the script callstack. Removing only that class let it load. Copy the shape you need into your own mesh instead. A mod mesh may still name the game’s own textures.
  • Add-on meshes are placed from the truck’s zero, not from the socket’s frame. One hardtop built relative to the chassis bone drew a metre too low, inside the cab, and looked for a day as if it did not draw at all.
  • Every add-on mesh needs the usual frames: a RootNode, a cdt collision frame, then the mesh. One roll cage listed in the garage with a price but could not be bought and did not draw; its meshes lacked the frames every working add-on has. Adding them is the fix we use now.
  • A texture with alpha must be saved with alpha. A __d_a texture written as plain RGB drew a banner as a white strip.
  • Another mod’s classes resolve only inside that mod. The game files every mod class under its mod’s id, so a truck that names another mod’s wheel set in CompatibleWheels never finds it. Copy the wheel classes, meshes and textures into your own mod. Then keep each tyre’s inner sidewall where the truck’s own tyre’s is: borrowing another truck’s wheel offset put one set of tyres 16 cm too far in, with the hubs through the wheel faces.
  • Engines, gearboxes and suspensions take no requirements. Only add-ons accept RequiredAddon or RequiredSuspension. “This lift needs the V8” cannot be enforced; the parts stay separate.
  • A dented corner may be the game, not your mesh. Trucks take visual damage. Almost every stock add-on, and many stock trucks, set AcceptVisualDamage="false" on their PhysicsModel. Compare a garage shot before hunting for a fault in a mesh that looks crumpled in the field.

Supply gear#

Parts that carry supplies (fuel cans, a parts case, batteries, a tent with camp kit) are add-ons whose <TruckData> adds capacity:

<TruckData FuelCapacity="45" RepairsCapacity="150" ElectricChargeCapacity="15" />

<TruckData> on an add-on accepts only a short list of fields: fuel, water, repairs, wheel repairs, electric charge, the metal detector, radar, wheel drone, cistern and kung flags. Anything else is ignored. That is why some things cannot follow a part: the engine ghost, for one, is read only from the truck (Placing the engine ghost).

Gear that is used up (IsConsumable="true") can hide its mesh as it empties, with FuelHide, RepairsHide and similar elements naming a frame and a threshold.

When the truck itself changes#

  • A lift kit is a suspension set, and a suspension set can swap the whole truck for a truck replacement (TruckReplacement on the set, a class in classes/truck_replacements/). The replacement can carry its own axles, drive shafts, engine ghost and wheel track. Stock replacements carry almost none of the base truck’s add-on sockets, and they do not need to: add-on sockets come from the base truck.
  • Big tyres need the lift. MaxWheelRadiusWithoutSuspension on the truck’s suspension socket locks tyres above that radius behind any suspension set other than the default.
  • A spare cannot follow the fitted tyres. A spare-wheel add-on draws a fixed mesh, and RequiredAddon resolves only against add-ons, not wheels. Offer one spare per tyre size and let the player pick.

Never pull a class a save is using#

A save stores every fitted mod add-on as <class>_<mod id>. Remove that class from the mod and the save names something that no longer exists. Before a rebuild drops any add-on class, scan the saves (read only) and keep every fitted class, with its mesh and texture, until the player has taken the part off in the garage.

What has been checked#

Confirmed in game on a built truck or a port: the socket offset, requirements, supply capacities, the per-size spares, the loading-screen freeze and its fix, the hardtop drawn a metre low, and other mods’ wheel sets once copied in. The TruckData field list was read from the game’s executable. The frame-name fault was found by reading the converted meshes; the parts drew once the names matched, but that fix was not checked on its own. Not yet confirmed: that the missing frames alone stopped the roll cage, that the alpha flag alone fixed the banner, whether AcceptVisualDamage="false" keeps a custom truck’s body clean, and gear hiding as it empties on a custom part.
