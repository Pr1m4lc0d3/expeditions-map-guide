Custom licence plates

How a truck can wear your own licence plate. The plate is an add-on that covers the plate the truck already carries, and one converted mesh can show any number of different plates.

This page is draft. It may be incomplete or unverified. Anything marked unverified has not been proven in a running game.

Custom licence plates#

A truck in the game carries a licence plate only if its maker modelled one. This page explains how your own plate can replace it, and which trucks that works on. The Doc’s Rocks Plate Maker (PC only, on mod.io) does all of this from a small Windows program.

The rule it follows: a truck that comes with no plate gets none. The custom plate only replaces a plate that is already there.

Where the plates are#

Trucks that came with the game. The plate is a real piece of the front bumper mesh, not a picture painted on the body. Most share two plate textures (license_plates_pack_01 and _02, the “EXPEDITIONS” plates). A few have a plate texture of their own. Each bumper that carries a plate has its own copy, so a truck with three bumpers has three plates in three places.

Truck mods. Every mod does it its own way. Some reuse the stock bumpers, plates included. Some model a plate piece into their own body. Some have no plate at all.

Why the plate is a cover#

An add-on cannot hide part of another mesh. So the custom plate is a thin plate mesh that sits a few millimetres in front of the stock plate and covers it. It has the same outline, faces the same way, and has its edges folded back so it reads as one solid plate from the side.

That plate is an add-on like any other. It fits a slot on the truck and names the bumper it belongs to:

<GameData Category="fender_front" Price="100">
    <RequiredAddon Types="harvester_scout_800_front_bumper_default" />
    <InstallSocket Type="LicensePlateHarvesterScout800" />
</GameData>

RequiredAddon makes the plate fittable only while its own bumper is on the truck. It works from a mod’s add-on to a bumper that came with the game. The other bumpers’ versions are still listed in the garage, but can’t be fitted.

One mesh, any number of plates#

Converting a mesh needs the editor’s converter for every model, which is slow. The plate avoids it.

A converted mesh starts with a small text header that names its textures:

AlbedoMap="trucks/platemaker_a__d.tga"

Each plate bumper is converted once, as a master. A new plate is a copy of that master with one change: the texture name in the header now points at the new plate’s own picture. The shape, the mapping and everything else stay byte for byte the same. Only the plate picture itself goes through the game’s texture converter.

Adding the slot#

Trucks that came with the game keep their classes in the game’s own initial.pak, and a mod cannot add a slot to them. As with rock lights, that needs a patcher on your own PC, which is why the Plate Maker is PC only.

A truck mod keeps its classes in its own pak, and a mod’s add-ons only work inside that same mod. So on a mod truck, the slot and the plates go into that mod’s own files. The slot copies the ParentFrame and Offset of the slot its bumper fits in, so the plate sits where the bumper sits.

Mod updates

Updating a truck mod from mod.io replaces its paks, and its plates go with them. Put them back afterwards. If the update moved the bumper’s slot, the plate would float, so the Plate Maker skips that truck instead.

Which trucks#

  • Trucks that came with the game: 38 carry a plate on at least one front bumper. Many plates are tilted back or turned at an angle, such as the Tuz 16’s reinforced bumper, which has its plate on the driver’s corner. So each plate is measured in its own plane, with its long edge setting the picture’s direction. The Haukka NS420 has a full-size plate only on its standard bumper; its other two bumpers show a 6.6 cm strip, too thin for a plate. The Shiba Overlander’s light-coloured front part is a grille, not a plate.
  • Truck mods: the same method works inside a truck mod’s own files, over the plate that truck already carries: a stock bumper it reuses, or a plate piece of its own. A truck mod with no plate gets none.

Sharing what you made#

Changing a truck on your own PC is yours to do, whoever made it. Uploading someone else’s truck with plates added is not, unless its author says you may or its licence allows it.

The state plate designs belong to the states that issue them. The Plate Maker fetches them from blanklicenseplates.com on your own PC and does not ship them.

What has been checked#

  • In game: custom plates on the stock bumpers of trucks that came with the game (Scout 800, DON 71), with only the fitted bumper’s version buyable.
  • In game: one master mesh showing two different plates by changing only the texture name.
  • In game: plates on a truck mod, on two plate spots of its own.
  • Not yet checked in game: the tilted and turned plates, and two plates on one truck at the same time.
