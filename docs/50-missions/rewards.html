Missions

Rewards

Money, trucks, specialists, FOB modules and your own custom content. Plus the reward classes that look fine and never pay.

Two places to attach a reward#

  • on the objective, paid when the whole thing is finished
  • on each stage, paid the moment that stage closes

Both draw from the same list of reward types. Stage rewards are the more interesting of the two, because they can change the map while the player is still on it. See building a story.

What you can give#

Reward Gives the player
ObjectiveRewardMoney cash documented
ObjectiveRewardUnlock a truck or upgrade becomes purchasable documented
ObjectiveRewardItem a truck or upgrade, handed over outright documented
ObjectiveRewardSticker a truck sticker documented
ObjectiveRewardSpecialist a crew member becomes available documented
ObjectiveRewardBaseUpgrade a FOB module is unlocked documented
ObjectiveRewardOpenZoneProperties a new zone appears, with properties you define documented
ObjectiveRewardUpdateZoneProperties an existing zone changes, gaining or losing properties documented
ObjectiveRewardOpenZoneDesc the map text and icons for a newly revealed zone schema only
ObjectiveRewardExperience experience schema only
ObjectiveRewardOverlayState changes an overlay’s state, such as a road surface or a bridge deck schema only
ObjectiveRewardZoneInventoryRandModifier alters what a zone randomly spawns schema only
ObjectiveRewardsByTime rewards gated on elapsed time schema only

The last five are absent from the official guides entirely. ObjectiveRewardOverlayState is the most immediately useful of them, because it means the visible state of a road or bridge can change as a reward.

Rewards that never pay

Several reward types are technical scaffolding or are marked for removal. They are accepted, they look correct in the file, and they do nothing:

ContestResultRewards, ObjectiveRewardAddItemsToZone, ObjectiveRewardsUpgradeGarage, ObjectiveSingleRewardUnlockOnce, ObjectiveRewardSettingsBase, ObjectiveRewardListener, ObjectiveRewardTrialPartItem

ContestResultRewards is the one that bites, because it takes an amount and a timeLimit and therefore looks exactly like a working money reward. If it is an objective’s only reward, that objective pays nothing.

Rewarding your own custom content#

Short answer: yes, for all three of the things people usually want.

Your own devices and items#

Works. A custom inventory item has an id, and the objective system takes custom ids the same way it takes built-in ones. You can require them, ask the player to collect them, and hand them over as rewards with ObjectiveRewardItem.

One requirement that will stop your expedition dead

If a custom item appears in an expedition’s requiredInventory, that item must have canTakeInHq enabled. documented

Without it the player cannot load the item at Headquarters, so they can never satisfy the requirement, so they can never start the expedition. The expedition simply refuses, and nothing explains why.

Your own bases#

Works. ObjectiveRewardBaseUpgrade takes a FOB module id, and custom FOB modules are a supported, documented entity. The built-in ids are:

BASE_MODULE     SUPPLY_SHOP    WORKSHOP       SERVICE_STATION   GAS_STATION
REPAIR_ZONE     PARKING        GENERATOR      ANTENNA           HELICOPTER
DRONE_BASE      TOW_TRUCK      EXTRA_STORAGE  DOUBLE_STOCK      NOTE

This is a good reward for a mid-mission stage. “Get the generator running” that actually unlocks the generator module reads as real progress.

Your own specialists#

Works for the built-in crew. Probably works for yours. unverified

ObjectiveRewardSpecialist takes an id, and the documented examples are built-in crew like Mechanic_01, Jaeger_02 and Hydrologist_03.

Custom specialist descriptors do carry an isLocked flag, which is exactly the mechanism such a reward would open, and it defaults to unlocked so a custom crew member is normally available from the start anyway.

What is not established is the id string a custom specialist answers to. The descriptor file contains no id field at all, and identity appears to come from the pak’s own name. So this is likely to work and the exact spelling is a guess. Test it before you build a mission whose payoff is a custom crew member.

Bonus rewards#

Extra money for playing well, rather than for finishing. Expeditions only, not contracts or tasks.

Each one carries a description and an amount, and most carry a limit. The engine has twenty conditions. The official guides cover twelve.

Documented Undocumented schema only
BonusConditionDamageTaken BonusConditionCompleteExpedition
BonusConditionNoRecovery BonusConditionDriveLessThenLimit
BonusConditionEngineStall BonusConditionSpendFuelLessThenLimit
BonusConditionTireInflation BonusConditionSpendFuelMoreThanLimitInOneGo
BonusConditionItemConsumed BonusConditionFinishExpWithEmptyInventory
BonusConditionDoNotUseItem BonusConditionFinishExpOnSpecificTruckType
BonusConditionDetectResource BonusConditionDontSkipTime
BonusConditionMinigameTryLimit BonusConditionHaveMoreItemsThanLimit
BonusConditionDriveXMetersOnLowTiresInOneGo
BonusConditionCollectMoreItemsThenLimitOnExp

Several of the undocumented ones are more interesting than the documented ones. “Finish with an empty inventory”, “finish on a specific truck type” and “do not skip time” are all real design levers. At least one undocumented condition is already in use by a published custom map, so the group is not dead code.

BonusConditionEngineStall deserves a note: it counts engine stalls for a given reason, and the base game mostly uses it with ROLL_OVER. So the natural reading of it is “do not roll your truck”.

Gating an expedition before it starts#

Three lists decide whether a player may begin an expedition at all. All three are expeditions only.

List Requires
requiredInventory items, by type or id, in the stated quantity
requiredSpecialities specific crew
requiredTruckAddons SEISMIC_VIBRATOR, METAL_DETECTOR or SIDEBOARD

These are good for making a mission feel prepared for rather than stumbled into. Use them sparingly, because a requirement the player cannot work out how to satisfy just looks like a broken map.
