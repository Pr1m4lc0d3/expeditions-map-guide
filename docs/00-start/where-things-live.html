Where everything lives

Your map exists in three places at once and they drift apart. This is the single most common source of wasted days, so it comes before anything else.

Your map is not one thing in one folder. It exists in three places, they hold different data, and they fall out of step with each other constantly.

Almost every “I fixed it and nothing changed” story ends here. So does almost every “it worked yesterday”. Learn this page and you will skip a category of problem entirely.

The three copies#

All three live under Documents\My Games\Expeditions\Media\.

Folder What it is Who writes it Who reads it
prebuild\<level>\ The source. Your real work lives here The editor, when you save The editor, when you open the map
levels\<Level>\ The staging copy. What the zone settings tool shows you, and what the packer reads The editor and the zone tool The packer
Mods\<Level>.pak The product. A zip archive The packer The game

Read that last column again. The game only ever reads the .pak. Nothing you do in prebuild or levels changes what you see when you press Deploy until you pack again.

The mistake everyone makes once

You find a bad value, you fix it in the .pak because that is the file the game reads, and it works. Then you pack the map again and your fix is gone, because the pack was built from the source, which you never touched.

Fix the source. Then pack. Editing the pak is for emergencies, not for work you want to keep.

The flow, in one direction#

     you, in the editor
             │  save
             ▼
   prebuild\<level>\          ← the source. edit here.
             │  the editor copies forward
             ▼
   levels\<Level>\            ← staging. the zone tool reads this.
             │  Pack (about 25 minutes)
             ▼
   Mods\<Level>.pak           ← the game reads only this.

It flows one way. There is no “sync back”.

Closing the editor is a write, not a stop#

This one costs people a whole evening.

While the editor has your map open, it holds the entire map in memory. When you close it, it writes that memory back over your files. So if you edit prebuild by hand while the editor is open, closing the editor silently throws your edit away.

The safe order is always:

1
In the editor: **save**, then **close it**.
2
Make your file edits now, with the editor shut.
3
Open the editor again. It reads your edits.
4
Pack.

Packing rewrites some of your work#

The pack step is not a simple copy. It rebuilds parts of your map from what the editor has in memory, and one of the things it rebuilds is the list of zones.

Zone properties can be discarded during a pack. The zone names survive, because they come from the objects placed in your scene, but the settings you attached to those names can be regenerated as an empty list. If you pack and your deploy zone stops working, this is the first thing to check, not the last.

The habit that protects you: after every pack, open the packed file and confirm your zones still have properties. It takes seconds and it catches a failure that otherwise costs you a whole test cycle.

Zone names: spaces in the source, underscores in the pack#

A genuinely nasty one, and it is worth understanding now rather than discovering it three weeks in.

  • The editor matches a zone’s settings to the object in your scene by name, and scene names contain spaces. Rename them with underscores in the source and the editor stops finding them, so your settings silently detach.
  • The game splits one of its internal zone references on whitespace. A zone whose name contains a space can never be looted and can never receive delivered items.

No single spelling satisfies both. So:

The rule

Spaces in the source. Underscores in the pack.

Keep your source names as the editor made them, with spaces. After packing, convert the names of any zone that holds or receives items to underscores. Every time you pack.

This affects storage zones, airdrops and construction zones. It does not affect a zone that is only a deploy point, because nothing passes through it.

Cloning a map#

Copying a finished map to start a new one is a good habit with one trap in it.

A map’s identity is a token stored in user_name_of_map.txt, and a copy still holds the original’s name. Two maps then claim to be the same map, and the game loads whichever it finds first.

The symptom is bewildering and worth memorising, because you will never guess the cause from it: “this is an older version of the map I just packed.”

If you clone a map, change the identity in every file that carries it, or do not clone at all.

What to take away#

  • edit prebuild, never the pak
  • close the editor before hand-editing, because closing it overwrites
  • the game only sees the pak, so nothing counts until you pack
  • check your zone properties survived the pack
  • convert item-carrying zone names to underscores after packing
  • a cloned map needs its own identity
